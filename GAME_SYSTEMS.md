# Game systems and what they need from a star map

Checked 2026-09-24 against publisher pages. This is a reading list and a description of each game's world format. It does not reproduce rule text, and it is not a translator from one game's code to another.

- `C:\Users\DougJ\Documents\GitHub\jumpgate_starroute\GAME_SYSTEMS.md`

**Jumpgate Starroute** stores routes between stars, faction stance, and a thin system card (high concept, tags, port, services, imports, exports).

**Orbit Match** stores bodies, orbits, and the transfer that arrives matched. Stat-heavy world lines are filled there, by hand at first.

Publishers move pages. If a free download disappears, start from the publisher's home and look for the starter named here.

## Which facts live where

| Fact | Star map | Orbit Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catalog star, coordinates, light-years, spectral type | yes | reads them |
| Routes, gates, lanes, a short drive's range, or both | yes | no |
| Hop time, fuel, toll, dock time on an interstellar route | yes | no |
| Faction stance (−3 to +3) | yes | no |
| High concept of the star, port, services, imports, exports | yes | may display |
| Planets, moons, belts, stations, orbits | pointer only | yes |
| Gravity, atmosphere, water, day length, size | no | yes |
| Population, government, tech level, law, as body facts | no | yes |
| Transfer time and fuel inside the system | no | yes |

A tag such as `water world` on the star map is a label someone typed. A water world with an orbit is Orbit Match.

## Traveller, Mongoose, and Cepheus

These share the Universal World Profile. The profile is the **mainworld**, one short code. In the order on the [Traveller SRD world-creation page](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/world-creation/): starport, size, atmosphere, hydrographics, population, government, law level, then tech level. `C763644-9` is a routine port, a small world, a standard tainted atmosphere, a wet world, millions of people, a captive government, moderate law, and an early interstellar tech level. Each position is one digit, 0–9 and then A upward.

The longer sector line adds the hex, bases, trade codes, an amber or red zone, a three-digit PBG (population multiplier, planetoid belts, gas giants), allegiance, and spectral type. Mongoose's World Builder's Handbook and Traveller5 go on to the rest of the system: other stars, orbits, moons. Classic, Mongoose, Cepheus, and Traveller5 use the same shape. Tech level and law differ in the details between editions.

The profile is a body. It belongs in Orbit Match as plain facts, with a later export if anyone wants the letters. The hex map and the jump routes belong on the star map. This tool's routes are real 3D light-years, not parsec hexes. A 16 light-year cap is about 5 parsecs, in the range of Jump-2 through Jump-5, and it will not drop onto a blank subsector sheet without stretching.

There is no Mongoose Traveller 3rd edition as of this check. Mongoose is still revising 2nd edition. Cepheus is not a Traveller edition.

## GURPS 4e

- **Home:** [Steve Jackson Games, GURPS](https://www.sjgames.com/gurps/). The space book is [*GURPS Space*](https://gurps.sjgames.com/space/) (4th edition; a 2022 printing is the current one).
- **Free start:** [*GURPS Lite*](https://www.sjgames.com/gurps/lite/), 32 pages, free to copy. Character creation, success rolls, and combat. No world generation.
- **Community:** [GURPS Wiki](https://gurps.fandom.com/wiki/GURPS_Space) (fan-written, and it says so) and the [SJ Games forums](https://forums.sjgames.com/).

*GURPS Space* uses a planetary record, not a code. The steps cover world type, atmospheric pressure and composition, hydrographic percentage, climate, diameter, density, surface gravity, resources, settlement, tech level, population, society type, Control Rating (0–6), economics, and bases. A second sequence builds the star: mass, luminosity, and orbits. Interstellar campaigns usually sit around TL9–11. Gravity is a number in Gs because encumbrance uses it.

There is no official compact string. Those body facts belong in Orbit Match. Light-year distances and who can reach whom belong on the star map. GURPS can play the map as gates or as a drive. The campaign tariff is where the table writes light-years per day or fuel per parsec in their own units.

## Stars Without Number

The published title is **Stars Without Number**. Kevin Crawford, Sine Nomine Publishing.

- **Home:** the Sine Nomine catalog on [DriveThruRPG](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/publisher/3482/sine-nomine-publishing).
- **Free start:** [*Stars Without Number: Revised Edition*, free version](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/230009/stars-without-number-revised-edition-free-version). Most of the game, including sector creation, world tags, factions, and spike drives. The paid book adds transhuman play, heroic characters, space magic, and true AI.
- **Community:** no single official wiki. The free book is the reference. One long-running fan tool is [hamhambone's sector viewer](https://hamhambone.github.io/swn/).

A world is a short block: atmosphere, temperature, biosphere, population, tech level, and one or two **world tags**. Each tag is a situation (abandoned colony, megacorps) with suggested enemies, friends, complications, things, and places. The revised free book also has other points of interest in the system, and a separate faction layer. The sector is an 8-by-10 hex map. A spike drive rated 1 through 6 crosses that many hexes. In the default setting the old jump gates are already dead, which is why the drive exists. TL4 is ordinary interstellar "postech." Pretech is lost Mandate technology, usually a tag or a ruin.

The world block is Orbit Match, or even just tags on the star card if the table wants to stay light. The hex adjacency is the star map's problem, and real 3D distances will not lay onto that hex sheet without distortion. Faction stance on the star map is the same *kind* of fact as SWN's faction layer, at a coarser grain.

## Savage Worlds (Adventure Edition)

- **Home:** [Pinnacle Entertainment Group](https://peginc.com/). The shop is [shop.peginc.com](https://shop.peginc.com/).
- **Free start:** the [Savage Worlds Test Drive](https://peginc.com/product/deadlands-the-weird-west-blood-on-the-range-savage-worlds-test-drive-swade/) is free and complete for one session. It is a Deadlands adventure, so it teaches the dice and does not teach planets. [New to Savage Worlds](https://shop.peginc.com/pages/new-to-savage-worlds) points at it.
- **Space book:** the *Science Fiction Companion* for the Adventure Edition (2024–2025) is the genre book. It includes a random planet generator. Reviews describe that chapter as a generator of planets, not a positional code and not a full economy worksheet.
- **Community:** Pinnacle's forums, linked from the new-player page. The core rules are not an open reference.

A Savage Worlds world is a description plus a few traits that turn into setting rules or hazards. The star card's high concept, tags, and faction cover a session. Body details, if wanted, are Orbit Match.

## Fate Core

- **Home:** [Evil Hat](https://evilhat.com/).
- **Free start:** Fate Core and Fate Accelerated are pay-what-you-want. The readable rules are the [Fate SRD](https://fate-srd.com/). Sheets and the version guide are on [Evil Hat's Fate downloads](https://evilhat.com/fate-downloads/). The [Fate System Toolkit](https://evilhat.itch.io/fate-system-toolkit) is also pay-what-you-want. Fate Condensed is the shorter current text of the same engine.
- **Space book:** the [*Fate Space Toolkit*](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/266197/fate-space-toolkit) (2019, paid) covers travel models, fights, and alien worlds as aspects.

Fate Core has no planetary statistics. A world is a high concept, a trouble, and other aspects. That is the star card.

The Fate game with a compact system line is **Diaspora** (VSCA Publishing, 2009), which was not on the original outline. Each system is three numbers from −4 to +4: Technology, Environment, Resources. Systems connect only along slipstreams, which are fixed faster-than-light routes. Those routes are the star map. The three numbers can sit as tags or as Orbit Match fields if a table wants them. The old publisher SRD is on the Internet Archive; `vsca.ca` has gone quiet.

## Coriolis

Free League publishes two games under this name.

**Coriolis – The Third Horizon** (2017) is a web of thirty-six star systems linked by ancient portals. The game page is [freeleaguepublishing.com/games/coriolis](https://freeleaguepublishing.com/games/coriolis/). Free League treats it as the previous game: print is winding down, and the PDF remains on DriveThruRPG. The free [*Third Horizon* quickstart](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/204702/coriolis-the-third-horizon-quickstart) has character creation, skills, combat, and the scenario *Dark Flowers*.

Its planet generator is in the *Atlas Compendium*. Size, orbit, atmosphere, temperature, geosphere, population, and spaceport, then a scenario hook and which faction is present. The core book already names the thirty-six systems. There is no positional code. Recreating that portal web is a star-map job: delete generated routes, draw the portals by hand. The planet rows are Orbit Match.

**Coriolis: The Great Dark** is the current Free League science-fiction game, a sequel with a different map. Home: [freeleaguepublishing.com/games/coriolis-the-great-dark](https://freeleaguepublishing.com/games/coriolis-the-great-dark/). Play starts in one system, Jumuah, and expeditions go outward. A free quickstart (setting primer, basic rules, and *The Sky Machine*) shipped with the crowdfunding campaign. One home system plus destinations is "both" or "range" on the star map, with Jumuah's moons and ports in Orbit Match.

## Scum and Villainy

**Scum and Villainy** is a Forged in the Dark game from Evil Hat. The [Blades in the Dark](https://bladesinthedark.com/) site has the free engine reference. Scum and Villainy itself is a separate book. The old public beta is obsolete.

The Procyon sector is a handful of named systems connected by jumpgates and hyperspace lanes. Planets are places with factions, heat, and jobs. There is no size, atmosphere, population, or tech digit. The star map's routes, holders, and stance are the whole interstellar picture. A high concept is the planet. Orbit Match is optional color.

## Mothership

- **Home:** [Tuesday Knight Games](https://www.tuesdayknightgames.com/) and [mothershiprpg.com](https://www.mothershiprpg.com/).
- **Rules:** 1st edition *Player's Survival Guide* (about 44 pages, paid) plus the *Warden's Operations Manual*. The Survival Guide is the short rules text. It is not a free full download.
- **Free:** [character sheets, a printable Warden screen, and a Basic Training course](https://www.tuesdayknightgames.com/pages/mothership-resources-downloads), plus a companion app.
- **Community:** the publisher's Discord, linked from those sites.

Mothership has no star-system or planetary statistics. Jobs are modules: a derelict, a moon, an outpost. The star map answers which star the next job is on. The module is the horror. A body in Orbit Match is only there if the table wants a moon to fly to. A world profile would fight the game.

## Starfinder

Paizo. The current edition is **Starfinder 2nd edition** (2025). First edition remains online. 2nd edition is the revision. There is no third edition.

- **Home:** [paizo.com/starfinder](https://paizo.com/starfinder). 2nd edition rulebooks, including the *Galaxy Guide* (May 2025), are in the [Paizo store](https://store.paizo.com/starfinder/starfinder-second-edition/rulebooks/digital-editions/).
- **Free rules:** [Archives of Nethys, 1st edition](https://www.aonsrd.com/) and [2nd edition](https://2e.aonsrd.com/). In July 2026 Paizo and the Archives confirmed that the Archives stay the official rules reference.
- **Community setting wiki:** [starfinderwiki.com](https://starfinderwiki.com/).

The 1st edition gazetteer line, on the Archives system pages: diameter, mass, and gravity as multiples of lost Golarion (Earth), atmosphere in words, length of the day, and length of the year. Authors write these. They are not rolled, and the order is not a code. Those are body facts for Orbit Match.

2nd edition *GM Core* discusses shape, landmass, Near Space, and the Vast. Travel in the setting is the Drift: many destinations, time depending on how charted the region is. A fixed gate network is a different premise. The star map can still hold the roads a ship takes when it is not in the Drift. Drift times are a campaign tariff the table invents. They are not this catalog's light-years unless the table wants them to be.

## What the star map already records

Per linked star today: catalog name, coordinates in light-years, distance from Sol, spectral type when NASA has it, a count of known planets, a ranking score, a culture and nation, whether it is a hub, and how many gates it is from Sol. Each route is a pair of stars and a length in light-years. The fuller catalog also has stellar mass, radius, temperature, and counts of rocky, gas-giant, and ice-giant planets, plus this setting's habitat bands (carbon, sulfur, silicon).

It does not record atmosphere, gravity, hydrographics, population, government, law, or tech level. Planet count is how many planets NASA has published, which favors large, close-in worlds. The habitat bands place peoples in the Turquenish setting. They are not atmospheres, trade codes, or world tags.

Shipped neighborhoods are about 60 light-years across, with jump caps of 16, 28, and 36 light-years. A link means a route exists under that cap.
