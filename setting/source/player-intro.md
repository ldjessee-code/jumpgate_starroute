# Player briefs (from the tabletop notes)

Plain-language copy of `player-intro.rtf`. These briefs are for the **crowded**
Turquenish neighborhood and the **sparse** variant. The lonely-humans pack
keeps the human factions and drops the alien neighbors.

## Turquenish Empire

A prosperous, visionary society. They enhance life through biotechnology:
genetic editing for health, longevity, and adaptability, for themselves and
their descendants. Wealth went into **O’Neill colonies** — rotating cylinder
habitats with bioengineered ecosystems, engineered crops, and bio-recyclers.

After the war, frontier colonies risk isolation if gate traffic falls. Some
citizens still argue about genetic “purity” laws; most of those laws have
been struck down. The Empire funds trade missions to keep Faetheren gates
busy.

A Turquenish crew member might be a genetically enhanced pilot, a biotech
engineer, or a diplomat trying to hold the neighborhood together.

## Mardat Coalition

Smaller population, higher automation. They prefer **mechanical and
electronic** improvement: cybernetic implants, robotics, Near-AIs (NAIs)
running factories, trade nets, and compact habitats. Fewer giant colonies,
more asteroid bases where people plug into NAI-driven systems.

Core systems recovered fast on drones and NAIs. Frontiers are short on parts.
A Mardat crew member might be a cyber-technician, an NAI programmer, or a
convoy escort.

## Post-war trade

Ships like a Scarab-class frigate (often a former military hull) move biotech
supplies one way and cybernetic components the other. Established borders
still inspect: Turquenish customs hunt unapproved chrome; Mardat scanners
hunt smuggled gene-work. Overzealous checkpoints can starve a colony. Both
sides hire independents anyway, because unused gates get neglected.

Mixed Turquenish–Mardat crews are common after the war. Expect arguments in
the galley about spas versus integrators, and expect Bazzar fixers, Aboreal
couriers, and Schettel scavengers in the docks on the crowded and sparse
maps.
